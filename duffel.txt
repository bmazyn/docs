Brian pulls into the parking lot at 7:45 a.m. and stays in the car for a few minutes, looking around.

The bush is right where he remembers it. Nothing looks unusual. The food places and the Verizon store won't open for another couple of hours, and there's almost nobody around.

He gets out and walks toward the bush.

Nothing.

He keeps going past it, following the row of bushes before cutting around to the other side. He walks slowly along the back, looking underneath and between them.

Still nothing.

He makes another pass, this time looking into the woods behind the bushes.  What is he even looking for? Signs of drug money? A robbery? He isn't really sure what either of those would look like.

Nothing.  "Okay, I've been here too long."  He heads back to the Mazda.

It's almost nine, so he drives to the bank and makes his second deposit. Again, no questions. No strange looks. Nothing happens.  


He picks up a breakfast sandwich and heads home.

But he keeps thinking about the woods behind the bushes.  "Should I go back? Maybe just walk around the edge?"

Then it hits him. It's Saturday morning.  Saturday has always been his favorite day of the week. Most Saturdays he meets up with friends at the climbing gym and spends a few hours there.

"Man, all I can think about is this bag of money."   

Reasonably so, he figures. But at some point he has to get back to something resembling a normal life. He has work on Monday.

"Or do I?"

He could live off the cash for a long time. But it's 2015. Nobody lives on cash anymore.  He pulls out his wallet and looks inside.

Seventeen dollars

The same seventeen dollars has probably been sitting there for months. He can't even remember the last time he used an ATM.  Its all just credit cards, even all my bill just automatically come out of my bank account.

And what happens when he wants something expensive? Suppose he finally upgrades the seven-year-old Mazda. He can't just walk into a dealership, drop 250 hundred-dollar bills on a desk, and buy a car.

*Can I?*

Wouldn't that trigger some kind of audit? IRS thing? He has no idea.

"Well, damn. I need to research this."

And he's definitely going to work Monday. Hopefully it's a slow day. Maybe he can spend some time figuring out the rules for cash deposits and how all of that actually works.

But first there are still the woods behind those bushes.

He can't stop thinking about them.

Brian grabs a beer from the refrigerator. There are four in there, probably the same four that have been sitting there for months. He doesn't drink much. He'd stocked up on some alcohol when he moved into the townhouse six months ago, after the divorce, and had barely touched it since.

He drinks the first one.

Then another.

Over the next couple of hours, all four disappear.

By the fourth, he's finally getting tired.

Good.  Maybe tonight he'll actually sleep.

### Chapter 3

Brian wakes up Sunday morning and almost immediately thinks about the woods.

He has to go back. One more look behind those bushes.

When he pulls into the parking lot, he stops.

There must be twenty Volkswagen Beetles scattered around the lot. Some old, some new, several modified. One looks like it's sitting about an inch off the ground.

Some kind of VW Bug meetup.

"Oh shit. How am I going to look walking around those bushes now?"

He keeps driving.

Whataburger seems like a better idea.

Near the end of breakfast, he looks out and sees a bunch of Beetles driving past.

"Good. They're leaving."

Then another thought.

*I hope they're leaving and not going to get a bigger group.*

He finishes breakfast and drives back.

The parking lot is completely empty.
